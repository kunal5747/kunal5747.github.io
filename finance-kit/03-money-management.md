# Money Management — the discipline layer

Raising money is offense; this file is defense. Construction businesses
rarely die from bad projects — they die from good projects with
undisciplined cash handling.

## Account architecture (open these, use them only for this)

```
┌─ RERA collection A/c (per project) ── 70% of buyer money, by law,
│                                       only construction + land cost
├─ Project operating A/c (per project) ─ construction spend, supplier
│                                        payments, the other 30%
├─ Reserve A/c (business-wide) ──────── interest reserve + tax reserve.
│                                       NO cheques/UPI from this account.
├─ Partner drawings A/c ─────────────── fixed monthly draw per partner
└─ Personal accounts ────────────────── never touched by business flows
```

Rules that make it work:

- **Interest reserve**: when any fixed-return money comes in, move 6 months
  of its interest into the Reserve A/c immediately, before spending a rupee
  on the site. You are borrowing reputation; this account protects it.
- **Tax reserve**: sweep a % of every collection (CA sets the rate) same day.
  GST and advance tax dates don't move for slow buyers.
- **Fixed partner draws** (like a salary), not need-based withdrawals.
  Profit distributions happen only at defined events (see allocation
  agreement template) — this single rule prevents most family disputes.

## The caps (write them down, review monthly)

1. **Fixed-obligation cap:** total monthly interest+EMI across everything
   ≤ 40% of *worst-case* monthly collections (assume half your expected
   sales velocity). Profit-share money doesn't count — which is why you
   prefer it.
2. **Cross-project rule:** project B's escrow/collections never fund
   project A's hole. It violates RERA, and it converts one sick project
   into two dead ones. If A is stuck, fix A's funding at A's level —
   renegotiate, raise, or pause.
3. **Deposit-slice rule:** JV deposits and pre-approval costs come only
   from equity or profit-share money — never from OD, never from supplier
   credit, never from another project.

## The weekly sheet (20 minutes, non-negotiable)

One page, updated every Monday, seen by all three partners:

| Item | This week | 4-week outlook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cash across all accounts | | |
| Collections due / received | | |
| Supplier + labour payments due | | |
| Interest/EMI due | | |
| OD drawn / limit headroom | | |
| **Cost-to-complete vs cash-to-collect (per project)** | | |

That last row is the whole game: for each site, remaining money you must
spend vs remaining money you will definitely receive. The moment
cost-to-complete exceeds cash-to-collect + available limits, you have a
funding gap — found six months early, it's a phone call; found late, it's
a distress sale.

## Monthly close (with your accountant / LedgerFlow)

- Bank reconciliations for every account (this is what your LedgerFlow
  project automates — statements → Tally).
- Project-wise P&L, not one blended pot: each site carries its own costs,
  its own interest, its own margin. Blended books hide a sick project
  inside a healthy one until it's too late.
- Supplier ledger review: aging of what you owe. Credit is fuel, but 90+
  day overdues cost you your next project's pricing.
- Update the deal model (`jv-deal-model.html`) with actuals vs plan.

## Personal finance rules for the partners

- Each partner keeps 12 months of household expenses outside the business,
  untouchable. A promoter who *must* pull money out in a bad quarter makes
  bad business decisions.
- Personal guarantees are joint exposure: track every PG signed in a
  register (lender, amount, project, date, release condition) and actively
  get PGs released when loans close — banks don't do it automatically.
- Insurance before investments: term life on all three partners (sized to
  cover business debt + family), health cover, and contractor's
  all-risk + workmen compensation policies per site. One accident without
  WC cover can erase a project's margin.

## Signs you're drifting (act the week you see them)

- Interest reserve account gets "temporarily borrowed" for a site payment.
- Supplier payments slip from 60 to 90+ days as a habit, not an event.
- You're computing returns per month again instead of per annum.
- A new deal is justified by "the deposit is refundable anyway."
- The weekly sheet skips two Mondays in a row.
