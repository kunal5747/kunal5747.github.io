# Business Loans — how banks decide, and which loan for which job

## The menu, priced (indicative 2026 ranges)

| Product | Rate | Ticket | Speed | Use it for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Loan Against Property (LAP) | 9–12% | 50–65% of property value | 3–6 weeks | Cheapest large capital; needs NA/residential/commercial property |
| CGTMSE-backed MSME loan | 9–14% | up to ₹5 cr, **no collateral** (guarantee fee ~1%) | 4–8 weeks | Working capital/equipment when collateral is short |
| Bank term loan (secured) | 9–12% | per project/collateral | 4–8 weeks | Equipment, site development with clear repayment source |
| NBFC construction/project finance | 12–16% | 60–70% of construction cost | 4–8 weeks post-approvals | THE product for funding a sanctioned project; secured by project + receivables escrow |
| Unsecured business loan (NBFC/fintech) | 16–24% | ₹0.2–2 cr | days | Emergency bridge only — same price as private money with less flexibility |
| Mudra (Tarun Plus) | ~9–12% | up to ₹20 lakh | weeks | Small tickets, early entity |

## The three numbers in the banker's head

1. **DSCR (Debt Service Coverage Ratio)** = yearly cash available ÷ yearly principal+interest due. Banks want **≥ 1.25–1.5**. Before asking for any EMI-based loan, compute this yourself; if you're below 1.25, ask for longer tenure or a smaller amount — don't argue, restructure the ask.
2. **LTV** — they lend 50–65% of collateral value. You always bring margin.
3. **FOIR/leverage** — existing obligations vs income. Close/consolidate small expensive loans before applying for a big cheap one.

## Construction finance — the one built for you

Once a project has: registered JDA + sanctioned plan + RERA registration,
NBFCs/HFCs will lend 60–70% of remaining construction cost at 12–16%,
disbursed **in tranches against certified construction progress**, secured
by the project and an **escrow on the receivables** (which RERA already
forces — 70% of collections into the project account).

This is the "take-out" that makes expensive bridge money safe to use:
private money carries you from JDA signing to approvals; construction
finance + customer advances carry you from approvals to handover. Sequence
every deal this way and your blended cost of capital stays low even when
one slice is at 24%.

**What they'll ask for:** approved plans, RERA certificate, JDA, cost
budget + cash-flow (your `jv-deal-model.html` produces exactly this),
sales projections with comps, track record sheet, promoter contribution
already spent (your ₹5.05 cr *is* the promoter contribution story).

## Reading a sanction letter (negotiate these, in order of value)

1. **Rate reset terms** — EBLR-linked resets with repo; MCLR-linked resets slower. Ask which, and what spread.
2. **Pre-EMI / moratorium** — for project loans, interest-only during construction is standard; make sure it's in writing.
3. **Prepayment charges** — floating-rate loans to individuals/MSMEs: prepayment penalty is largely barred; get it stated as zero anyway.
4. **Processing + "other" fees** — legal, valuation, documentation, insurance-bundling. Everything is negotiable before sanction, nothing after.
5. **Covenants** — minimum turnover routing, no-further-borrowing clauses, personal guarantees. Know what you're signing; a PG from all three partners means all three estates are on the line.

## Government/scheme money worth checking each year

- **CGTMSE** limit raises (₹5 cr now) and reduced guarantee fees for women-led/SC-ST-led units — if any family member fits, structure for it.
- **PM Vishwakarma / state industrial policy** — occasional interest subventions for construction-allied trades.
- **Interest subvention on MSME loans** announced in budgets — your CA should check this every April.

## The order to build your debt stack

1. OD/CC limit (cheapest flexible) →
2. LAP on NA-converted land (cheapest bulk) →
3. CGTMSE if collateral runs out →
4. Construction finance per project (self-liquidating) →
5. Private money **only** for the slice none of the above can touch: the JV deposit/pre-approval bridge →
6. Unsecured NBFC loans: never as a plan, only as a 60-day emergency patch.
